Abstract

This work contributes to the understanding of high-dimensional fluid flows, focusing on quasi-geostrophic systems, by reconstructing turbulent flow fields from a partial, noisy, time-sequential set of tracer measurements. Lagrangian drifters stream local velocity rather than a gridded streamfunction; the inverse problem is to recover $\psi$ from those tracks. A spectral quasi-geostrophic twin generates truth and a forecast ensemble. Helmholtz inversion maps vorticity to streamfunction. A localized stochastic Ensemble Kalman Filter (WHERE) assimilates the noisy velocities, with an optional hybrid EnKF–particle-filter branch. The public lab is a runnable companion to the University of Calgary MSc thesis Data-driven filtering techniques for turbulent flow models (a Lagrangian data assimilation approach) (Droobi, 2025).

Method

1. Initialization

A spectral barotropic QG model on a periodic $[0, 2\pi]^2$ $\beta$-plane produces the truth and the forecast ensemble. Forty drifters sit on a jittered lattice and observe noisy velocities ($\sigma_o = 0.04$). Streamfunction is recovered from vorticity by Helmholtz inversion.

$$\partial_t q + J(\psi, q) + \beta \partial_x \psi = F - d q - \nu (-\nabla^2)^p q$$ $$q = \nabla^2 \psi - \mu \psi, \qquad \hat{\psi} = -\hat{q}/(\kappa^2 + \mu)$$

2. Localized analysis

WHERE is a stochastic EnKF on vorticity. Predicted velocities at the truth tracer sites form the observation operator. Gaspari–Cohn localization ($L_{\mathrm{loc}} = 1.55$) tapers the sample covariances; multiplicative inflation $\alpha = 1.08$ keeps the ensemble from collapsing. Analysis interval is $0.35$ time units on the committed run ($N_e = 24$).

3. Hybrid refinement

After the EnKF update, members can be reweighted by the Gaussian velocity likelihood and resampled when the effective sample size drops below $0.55\,N_e$. A $1.5\%$ jitter restores sample covariance after cloning. On this QG twin the hybrid matches large-scale correlation but does not beat WHERE on RMSE.
